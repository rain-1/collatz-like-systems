# fx10_64_s7: 5x+1 with one class of density 1/64 changed

**Rule.** T(n) = n/2 for even n, 5n+1 for odd n, **except** T(n) = 5n+5 for
n ≡ 10 (mod 64). T is not injective: T(1) = 6 = T(12).
Only the even class 10 (mod 64) is changed. There, n/2 is replaced by 5n+5,
which is the odd rule with offset 5.

**Questions.** FATE of 7. Which residue classes consist entirely of divergent
orbits?

| claim | status | tier |
|---|---|---|
| Every n ≡ 10, 20, 21, 42, 53, 55 (mod 64) has an unbounded orbit with infinitely many falls and a non-eventually-periodic itinerary | PROVED | 2 |
| The orbit of 7 enters that set at step 12 (183 ≡ 55), so it is infinite and not eventually monotone | PROVED | 2 |
| Every forward-closed union of residue classes, at any modulus 2^K, contains S; S is the least congruence trap | PROVED | 2 |
| No congruence divergence certificate exists, at any modulus, for any member of the same family at modulus 128 with acyclic G1 | PROVED (table in theorem4_precision.md) | — |
| Every n ≤ 20000 enters S or reaches the cycles of 1, 13 or 17 | EVIDENCE | — |

For unmodified 5x+1 the orbit of 7 is the classical unresolved case (believed
divergent). The change here touches density 1/64 of the integers. In the family
of single-class changes searched (theorem4_precision.md), it is the finest
change for which the method proves a non-monotone infinite orbit of 7.

## The trap

S = {10, 20, 21, 42, 53, 55} (mod 64). The residue graph mod 64 restricted to S
is as follows (all of it is exact; see `divlib.images`):

| class | branch | slope | images mod 64 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 5n+5 | 5 | 55 |
| 55 | 5n+1 | 5 | 20 |
| 20 | n/2 | 1/2 (fall) | 10, 42 |
| 42 | n/2 | 1/2 (fall) | 21, 53 |
| 21 | 5n+1 | 5 | 42 |
| 53 | 5n+1 | 5 | 10 |

No edge leaves S, so T(S) ⊆ S.

## Proof

* **No periodic point in S (Lemma A↑, theorem3_ascent.md).** The simple cycles
  of this graph are 10→55→20→10 (slope product 25/2), 42→21→42 (5/2) and
  10→55→20→42→53→10 (125/4). Every closed walk decomposes into simple cycles,
  so its product is > 1. All offsets are ≥ 0. A cycle x₀ = A x₀ + B with A > 1, B ≥ 0 is
  impossible for x₀ > 0. The check verifies this with the exact integer witness
  of Lemma A↑ rather than by hand. So every orbit entering S is not eventually
  periodic, hence unbounded.
* **Infinitely many falls (Lemma F).** The non-fall classes 10, 55, 21, 53
  induce the acyclic graph 53→10→55 (21 is isolated). So every 4 consecutive
  steps in S contain a halving.
* **Aperiodic itinerary (Lemma I).** Every closed walk contains at least one
  halving and only odd multipliers otherwise, so v₂(product) ≤ −1. Hence no
  relation T^h(x) = A x + B holds along an orbit from some point on, and the
  orbit has no monotone affine subsequence of the kind that makes tier-1 proofs
  possible.
* **Orbit of 7.** 7, 36, 18, 9, 46, 23, 116, 58, 29, 146, 73, 366, 183. The point
  183 ≡ 55 (mod 64) lies in S.
* **S is the least trap.** All numerators are odd and all denominators are
  powers of 2. The no-halving subgraph G1 mod 64 is acyclic: the only d = 1
  steps go odd → even, or 10 → 55 → 20, and 20 halves. So Lemma P applies:
  every nonempty forward-closed union of classes at any modulus 2^K contains a
  forward-closed union of classes mod 64, hence a sink component mod 64. The
  residue graph mod 64 has exactly one sink component, namely S.

## Why tier 2, and what is not settled

* Tier 0 cannot prove an infinite orbit.
* Tier 1 tricks fail. By Lemma I the orbit has no eventually periodic
  itinerary, so no affine pump. It has infinitely many falls, so it is not
  monotone. T is not injective, so non-return arguments do not apply.
* The proof is a 6-class congruence trap plus a finite table plus Lemma A↑,
  which is tier 2.
* **Not settled:** GLOBAL. The complement of S contains the cycles of 1, 13
  and 17. It also contains 2-adic expanding periodic points, for example the
  2-adic fixed point −1 of n ↦ (5n+1)/4 (orbit −1 → −4 → −2), whose classes 63, 60, 62 (mod 64) avoid S. By
  round 2's Lemma B, those points rule out a "capture-or-descend" certificate
  for the complement. Whether every orbit either enters S or cycles is OPEN.
  Every n ≤ 20000 does: 19320 enter S, and 382, 211, 87 reach the cycles of
  1, 13, 17 respectively.
