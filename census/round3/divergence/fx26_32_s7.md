# fx26_32_s7: 5x+1 with the class 26 (mod 32) sent to 7n+1

**Rule.** T(n) = n/2 for even n, 5n+1 for odd n, **except** T(n) = 7n+1 for
n ≡ 26 (mod 32). T is not injective, e.g. T(1) = 6 = T(12). The change destroys
the cycle of 13 (13 → 66 → 33 → 166 → 83 → 416 → 208 → 104 → 52 → 26 ↦ 183).

## Trap and proof

S = {5, 10, 20, 21, 23, 26} (mod 32). The residue graph on S is
5 → 26 (5n+1), 26 → 23 (7n+1), 23 → 20 (5n+1), 20 → {10, 26} (n/2),
10 → {5, 21} (n/2), 21 → 10 (5n+1). Nothing leaves S.

The simple cycles are (5, 26, 23, 20, 10), with product 175/4, then (10, 21) with
5/2, and (20, 26, 23) with 35/2. All products are > 1 and all offsets are ≥ 0,
so Lemma A↑ shows S has no periodic point.

The non-fall classes 5, 26, 23, 21 induce the acyclic graph 5 → 26 → 23, so
every 4 consecutive steps contain a halving (Lemma F). Every cycle contains a
halving and no other even factor, so v₂ < 0 and Lemma I gives an aperiodic
itinerary.

All numerators are odd. G1 mod 32 is acyclic, because the d = 1 path from 26 is
26 → 23 → 20 and 20 halves. S is the unique sink component mod 32, so Lemma P
makes S the least congruence trap at every modulus 2^K.

finer fx10_64_s7 supersedes it for task (3). It is kept because it is the
simplest example: six classes mod 32, with every certificate verified at the
modification's own modulus.
