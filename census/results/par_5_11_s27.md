# par_5_11_s27
Rule: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 5n+11. Seed 27.

## FATE: unknown — OPEN (tier 5)
Tried:

  random-parity model (1/3 log2 5 - 2/3 ~ 0.107). This is evidence of divergence, not proof.
- Periodic first-step separators: for every modulus M <= 3000, the forward closure of the residue
  class of T(27) = 146 in the residue graph mod M contains 27 mod M, so no congruence invariant

  that for a = 5 with gcd(5,d) = 1 such a separator exists at some modulus only when 5 | seed; 5 does not divide 27.)
- No conjugacy reduces it: gcd(27, 11) = 1, so the orbit does not sit in 11Z where the map is
  conjugate to 5n+1.
Reduces to: deciding whether one specific orbit of a 5x+d map is unbounded. This is the same kind of
problem as the open divergence question for 5n+1 orbits (e.g. the orbit of 7). No named equivalence.
Check (evidence only): census/checks/par_5_11_s27.py.

## GLOBAL: unknown — OPEN (tier 5)
Restriction to multiples of 11 is exactly conjugate to the 5n+1 map: for n = 11m, n even iff m even
(11 odd), n/2 = 11(m/2), and 5n+11 = 11(5m+1). So a "yes" here would prove every 5n+1 orbit is
eventually periodic, which is open and heuristically false (expected log-growth per odd step
log 5 - 2 log 2 > 0; e.g. the 5n+1 orbit of 7 appears to diverge). A "no" needs a proved divergent
orbit, which is not known for any map of this type. Congruence invariants, descent inequalities
and finite automata cannot settle it: the map has a/2^k drift > 1 on average, so no descent
argument exists, and the baseline shows many small starts with no cycle within budget.
