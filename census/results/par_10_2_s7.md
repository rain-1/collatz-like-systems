# par_10_2_s7
Rule: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 10n+2. Seed 7.

| question | answer | status | tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| FATE | unknown (apparently infinite) | OPEN | 5 |
| GLOBAL | unknown (conjecturally no) | OPEN | 5 |

## Reduction (tier 1, proved)
For odd n, 10n+2 = 2(5n+1) is even, so F(F(n)) = 5n+1. Hence the orbit of n under F
is exactly the orbit of n under the 5x+1 map T (n/2 even, 5n+1 odd) with the single
term 10n+2 inserted after each odd term. An F-orbit is eventually periodic iff the
corresponding T-orbit is. So:
- FATE(seed 7) is exactly the question whether the 5x+1 trajectory of 7 is bounded,
  a well-known open problem (7 is the smallest 5x+1 starting value believed to diverge).
- GLOBAL is exactly "every 5x+1 trajectory is eventually periodic", open; heuristically
  false (average growth factor sqrt(5)/2 > 1 per odd/even pair).

## What was tried
Exact simulation: 20000 steps, all terms distinct, current term ~470 digits (no cycle;
not a proof). No descent, congruence or automaton certificate can prove divergence here
without settling 5x+1 divergence; none is known. Note the reduction to 5x+1 is exact,
so this system *is* the 5x+1 problem.

## Referee
FATE: open-agreed (tier 5). GLOBAL: open-agreed (tier 5).
Checked independently that for odd n, 10n+2 = 2(5n+1), so F(F(n)) = 5n+1, and that F agrees with the 5x+1 map T on even n. The F-orbit is therefore the T-orbit with one extra term after each odd term, and the two orbits are eventually periodic together.
FATE is the boundedness of the 5x+1 orbit of 7, a standard open case. The 20000-step simulation is correctly labelled as evidence only.
GLOBAL is open in both directions. "Yes" would contradict the heuristic, and "no" would need a proof that some 5x+1 orbit diverges, which nobody has.
No tier 1-3 tool applies. Divergence cannot be certified by a finite automaton or a congruence invariant, because the parity sequence of a divergent orbit is not eventually periodic. An eventually periodic parity sequence would force a rational 2-adic point, which here means a cycle.
