# par_7_1_s3
Rule: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 7n+1. Seed 3.

## FATE: other-cycle or infinite (does NOT return) — OPEN between the two (tier 5); non-return PROVED (tier 2)
Non-return proof (congruence invariant mod 7). Let S = {1,2,4} mod 7, the subgroup generated by 2
in (Z/7)^*, i.e. the quadratic residues. If n is odd, 7n+1 = 1 mod 7, in S. If n is even,
n/2 = 4n mod 7 (4 = 2^{-1} mod 7), and 4 is in S, so S is preserved. Hence S is forward-closed.
T(3) = 22 = 1 mod 7 is in S, and 3 is not in S, so the seed never recurs.
(This uses that 2 is a square mod 7; it is the reason a = 7 admits a separator although 7 does not divide the seed.)

Cycle vs. infinite: OPEN. Simulation for 200000 steps finds no repetition (record at step 199981,
so preperiod + period > 199981) and the size reaches 54132 bits, matching the random-parity
growth rate (1/3 log2 7 - 2/3 ~ 0.27 bits/step). That is evidence, not proof. No closed formula,
conjugacy or descent applies; this is a specific instance of the open problem of proving any
7n+1 orbit unbounded.

## GLOBAL: unknown — OPEN (tier 5)
