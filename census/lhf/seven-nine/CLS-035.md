# CLS-035 — 7n+7, GLOBAL

Map T: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 7n+7. Question: is every positive orbit eventually periodic?

**Status: EQUIVALENCE (exactly CLS-032) + PARTIAL.**

**Equivalence (proof).** Let U be the 7n+1 map. (i) T(7m) = 7U(m) for every m ≥ 1: if m is even, both
sides equal 7m/2; if m is odd, T(7m) = 49m+7 = 7(7m+1). (ii) Halving preserves divisibility by 7, and
for odd n, T(n) = 7(n+1) ∈ 7Z. So 7Z is forward-invariant. Every orbit meets an odd number (halving
cannot continue forever), so every orbit enters 7Z. On 7Z, T is conjugate to U via m -> 7m. Hence
the T-orbit of n is eventually periodic iff the U-orbit of the m with T^j(n) = 7m is eventually periodic.
Conversely the T-orbit of 7m is 7 times the U-orbit of m, for every m ≥ 1. So GLOBAL(T) ⟺ GLOBAL(U) = CLS-032 (= CLS-034).

**Cycles.** Every T-cycle lies in 7Z, so the T-cycles are exactly 7·(U-cycles). The conjugacy maps
odd terms to odd terms (7m is odd iff m is). By Theorem C of CLS-032.md, the only T-cycle with at most 1000
odd terms is {7,56,28,14}.

**Theorem A′ (congruence obstruction).** If F is a nonempty union of residue classes mod M with T(F) ⊆ F,
then 7 ∈ F. Proof: the orbit of any element of F meets an odd n, and T(n) ∈ F ∩ 7Z. Let
G = {x > 0 : 7x ∈ F}. G is a union of classes mod M and nonempty, and U(G) ⊆ G since
T(7x) = 7U(x). By Theorem A (CLS-032.md), 1 ∈ G, so 7 ∈ F. ∎ So no congruence trap certifies a divergent 7n+7 orbit.

Check: `check_CLS-035.py` (exit 0): T(7m) = 7U(m) for m < 10^5; odd images lie in 7Z; reachability of the
class of 7 in every residue graph with even M ≤ 1000; the U-cycle enumeration; the cycle {7,56,28,14}.
