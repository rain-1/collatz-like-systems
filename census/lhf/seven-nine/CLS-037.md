# CLS-037 — 9n+1, GLOBAL

Map: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 9n+1. Question: is every positive orbit eventually periodic?

**Status: PARTIAL (GLOBAL remains OPEN).**

1. **Theorem C (q = 9): 9n+1 has no cycle with at most 1000 odd terms.** The proof is the exact enumeration
   argument of CLS-032.md. Any cycle with k odd terms has least odd term n with
   (9n+1)^k ≥ 2^{K_0} n^k, where K_0 is the least integer with 2^{K_0} > 9^k. All such n were tested
   exactly. An extra run with L = 3000 (all n ≤ 464416) also found no cycle.
   Since every cycle has an odd term, **every eventually periodic 9n+1 orbit ends in a cycle with more
   than 1000 (indeed more than 3000) odd terms, hence of length more than 2000 (6000).**
   Remark (an exact reformulation, not a resolution): GLOBAL = "no" follows at once if 9n+1 has no
   cycle at all. A non-eventually-periodic orbit takes infinitely many distinct values, so it is unbounded.
   Conversely, "no cycles" is the strongest form of "no". Proving that no cycle exists for every k is a
   Steiner / Simons–de Weger type Diophantine problem (n(2^K − 9^k) = Σ 9^{k−1−i} 2^{A_i}) and was not achieved.
2. **Theorem A (q = 9, CLS-032.md):** every nonempty forward-closed union of residue classes, at any modulus,
   contains 1. Hence (route (a)) no congruence trap certifies a divergent orbit. Note that 1 itself is not
   known to be periodic, so for 9n+1 this does not even say the trap contains a cycle. It says the trap
   contains the orbit of 1, whose fate is CLS-036.

Check: `check_CLS-037.py` (exit 0).

## Referee (adversarial check)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED with correction.** Theorem C for q=9 holds: no cycle has at most 1000 odd terms (independent re-run). The L=3000 claim was not independently confirmed in the referee's time budget; only L=1000 is verified. Theorem A for q=9 is correct as stated: every trap contains 1. But the sentence 'Hence (route (a)) no congruence trap certifies a divergent orbit' is NOT justified, and the caveat right after it contradicts it. Since 1 is not known to be periodic, Theorem A does not exclude a trap free of periodic points; if 9n+1 has no cycles, Z_{>0} is one. Corrected: every congruence trap contains the orbit of 1, so a route-(a) certificate would have to prove the orbit of 1 (CLS-036) unbounded. 'No cycles at all implies GLOBAL = no' is a correct but trivial reformulation. GLOBAL stays OPEN.

Independent test: `referee_check.py` (exit 0, about 55 s; written without sevnine_lib, uses the census/systems.py steps). It checks Theorem A at **every** modulus M <= 240 (odd moduli included, with the residue graph built from actual images of r+Mt), the Step-3 lemma directly on Z/m for all admissible m < 1500, and Theorem C with an independent implementation (B_k by binary search; the full, unaccelerated map). The author's check scripts also pass (exit 0, 3-9 s each).

Proof re-derivation, checked line by line and correct:
- Step 1, the 2-adic (or 3-adic) loss: q is invertible mod 2^{a-b}.
- Step 2: R_m is closed under h and h∘g, and hence under g, since h has finite order.
- Step 3: on Z/m', D^{-1} g D g^{-1} is x -> x - 1/2 (for m3_e, g_1 g_2^{-1} is x -> x + 1). On Z/p^e, g^j is constant for j >= e, and that constant is a unit in <2> (in <3> for m3_e), by LTE or primitivity.

Strengthening noted by the referee (not needed for the verdict): Theorem A also covers truncated traps F = {n >= N : n mod M in R}. Steps 1-3 go through for elements >= N, so F contains every large n = 1 mod m. It therefore contains some 2^j (3^j for m3_e), whose forward orbit reaches 1. Hence 1 is in F.

## Exclusion of cycles with up to seven local minima

**Theorem.** Under the shortcut map $S(x)=(9x+1)/2$ for odd $x$ and $S(x)=x/2$ for even $x$, every positive cycle, if one exists, has at least eight strict local minima in its least period. This excludes circuits of every length. The shortcut combines an odd step of the original map with its mandatory halving. It preserves cycles on reinserting the omitted even values. We count rising runs in the shortcut throughout.

**One run.** Start at an odd valley $x$. Let $a$ count successive odd steps, let $y$ be their even endpoint, and let $d$ count the subsequent halvings to the next odd valley $x'$. Then
$$
7y+1=(9/2)^a(7x+1),\qquad y=2^d x'.
$$
Indeed an odd step multiplies $7x+1$ by $9/2$, consuming exactly one power of two until the height is even. Thus $a=v_2(7x+1)$, where $v_2$ counts factors of two. In a circuit $x'=x$, giving the poll's formula
$$
x=\frac{9^a-2^a}{7(2^{a+d}-9^a)}.
$$
To handle several runs, we keep the exact endpoints in the first identity.

**The logarithmic error depends on the number of runs.** Assume a cycle with $r\le7$ runs. Denote the successive odd valleys by $x_i$, their peaks by $y_i$, the odd step counts by $a_i$, and the following even step counts by $d_i$. Start at the minimum $m=x_0$. Put $k=\sum a_i$ and $K=\sum(a_i+d_i)$. The run identity gives
$$
\frac{2^{a_i+d_i}x_{i+1}}{9^{a_i}x_i}
=\frac{1+1/(7x_i)}{1+1/(7y_i)}.
$$
Multiplying around the cycle cancels the valley ratios. Since $y_i>x_i\ge m$ and $\log(1+t)<t$ for $t>0$,
$$
0<\delta:=K\log 2-k\log9<\frac{r}{7m}\le\frac1m. \tag{1}
$$
Here and below $\log$ is natural unless a base is indicated.

**How many odd steps can fit?** Write $h_i=\log_2(7x_i+1)$ and $\alpha=\log_2 9$. We have $a_i\le h_i$ and
$$
h_{i+1}\le\log_2(7y_i+1)=h_i+(\alpha-1)a_i\le\alpha h_i.
$$
Hence $k\le h_0(\alpha^r-1)/(\alpha-1)<1800\log_2(7m+1)$, using $3<\alpha<16/5$ and $r\le7$. Also $K<4k$, since multiplying individual step ratios around the cycle gives $2^K=\prod_{x\text{ odd in the cycle}}(9+1/x)\le10^k$.

**A finite bound independent of the period.** Apply the explicit real-field form of Matveev's theorem to $1-3^{2k}2^{-K}=1-e^{-\delta}$. Unique factorization gives nonvanishing. The bases 3 and 2 lie in degree one, have admissible height parameters 2 and 1, and have exponent magnitudes below $4k$. The coefficient $1.4\cdot30^5\cdot2^{4.5}\cdot2$ is less than $2\cdot10^9$. Thus
$$
\exp[-2\cdot10^9(1+\log(4k))]<1-e^{-\delta}<\delta<1/m.
$$
Combining the resulting bound on $\log m$ with the bound for $k$, using $7m+1\le8m$ and $\log 2>1/2$, yields $k<10^{13}(1+\log(4k))$. Consequently $k<10^{15}$. The ratio $t/(1+\log(4t))$ increases for $t\ge1$, and $\log(4\cdot10^{15})<37$. [Matveev's theorem](https://doi.org/10.1070/IM2000v064n06ABEH000314) · [Explicit real-field form, Theorem 2](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_3181483_3/component/file_3181485/Ddamulira-Luca_On%2Bthe%2Bx-coordinates%2Bof%2BPell%2Bequations_2020.pdf)

Use the two reduced approximations $p/q$ to $\alpha$ in order:
$$
\frac{19763055687104648}{6234549927241963},
\qquad \frac{603988}{190537}.
$$
Each satisfies $|\alpha-p/q|<1/(2q^2)$. The checker verifies this with rational enclosures of the logarithms. Whenever $k<q$, the integer $Kq-kp$ is nonzero, so $|K-k\alpha|>1/(2q)$. Equation (1) gives $|K-k\alpha|<2/m$ and therefore $m<4q$. The first denominator exceeds $10^{15}$ and gives $7m+1<2^{58}$, hence $k<1800\cdot58=104400$. This is smaller than the second denominator. Its application gives **$m<762148$**.

**Exhausting the candidate minima.** Every positive cycle has an odd minimum because an even value immediately decreases. For each odd $m<762148$, the checker uses the original catalog map to follow at most seven whole rising runs and their descents. It rejects a candidate as soon as the next valley is smaller than $m$. A return to $m$ at any of those valleys would exhibit a cycle. Every run ends by the valuation identity, and every descent ends at the odd part of its peak. The exact scan of all 381,074 candidate minima produced no return. This completes the proof. [Reproducible checker](check_9x1_runs.py)

**Scope and precedent.** The argument places no upper bound on the length of an individual run. The assumption $r\le7$ enters both the error bound and the estimate on $k$ in terms of $m$. Cycles with more runs and the fate of the orbit of 1 remain open here. The method of bounding cycles with a prescribed number of minima by logarithmic forms and rational approximation was developed for generalized Syracuse maps by Simons. [Simons, *On the (non-)existence of m-cycles for generalized Syracuse sequences*](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa131-3-2)
