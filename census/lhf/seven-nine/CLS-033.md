# CLS-033 — 7n+1, FATE of 3

Map: even n -> n/2; odd n -> 7n+1. Question: does the orbit of 3 return to 3, enter another cycle,
or grow forever? (Non-return was already proved by the mod-7 invariant {1,2,4}.)

**Status: PARTIAL (the other-cycle vs. infinite question remains OPEN).**

Proved (full proofs in CLS-032.md):

1. **Restricted fate (Theorem C).** The only cycle with at most 1000 odd terms is {1,8,4,2}. So the
   orbit of 3 does one of three things: (a) grows without bound, (b) reaches 1, or (c) enters a cycle
   with more than 1000 odd terms, and that cycle's least odd term n satisfies
   (7n+1)^k ≥ 2^{K_0(k)} n^k for its odd-term count k.
2. **Congruence obstruction (Theorem A).** For every modulus M, the residue-graph forward closure of
   the class of T(3) = 22 contains the class of 1. So no congruence argument can exclude outcome (b).
   The mod-7 separator excludes only the return to 3 (3 ≢ 1,2,4 mod 7), and nothing of this kind can
   separate the orbit from the 1-cycle. Route (a), a forward-closed set of residue classes made of
   divergent orbits, is impossible.
3. "Grows forever" would imply that GLOBAL (CLS-034 = CLS-032) has answer "no". The converse fails.

Not proved: whether the orbit is unbounded. Simulation for 2·10^5 steps is evidence only.

Check: `check_CLS-033.py` (exit 0): residue-graph reachability for all even M ≤ 1000, cycle enumeration up to
1000 odd terms, and T(3) = 22 ≡ 1 mod 7.
