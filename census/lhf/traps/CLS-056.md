# CLS-056 (yah_avoid57): Yolcu-Aaronson-Heule Conjecture 4.14

**Map.** n = 5 or 7 mod 8 -> 1. Otherwise n/2 for even n and 3n+1 for odd n.

**Question.** Does every orbit enter {1,4,2}? Equivalently (YAH Conj. 4.14), does every C-trajectory that does not reach 1 contain some m = 5 or 7 (mod 8)?

**Status: PARTIAL (PROVED results below; the conjecture stays open).**

## Theorem 1 (sharp reformulation)
Let E = {n >= 1 : the C-trajectory of n contains no m = 5, 7 mod 8}. Every power of 2 lies in E. Conj. 4.14 holds iff E = {2^j : j >= 0}. In other words, any n in E that is not a power of 2 is a counterexample.

*Proof.* If n ∈ E reaches 1, consider the last odd value before 1 in the C-trajectory. Its successor 3m+1 is a power of 2, 2^{2k}. So m = (4^k - 1)/3 = 1 + 4 + ... + 4^{k-1}, which is = 5 mod 8 for k >= 2. Such an m is forbidden, so k = 1, m = 1, and the trajectory of n consists of halvings down to 1. Hence n is a power of 2. Conversely, the trajectory 2^j -> ... -> 1 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1 contains only 1, 2, 4 and multiples of 8, none of them 5 or 7 mod 8. ∎

(Check: the count of E ∩ [1,2^k] is exactly k+1 for k <= 20.)

## Theorem 2 (all but O(X^0.4057) integers satisfy the conjecture)
For X >= 2,

    #(E ∩ [1,X]) <= 2 a_L <= 2 + 12.4 X^{log2 ρ},   with L = floor(log2 X) and log2 ρ = 0.40569...

Here ρ = 1.324718... is the plastic number (ρ^3 = ρ + 1), and a_L is the number of binary words of length L with no factor 100 or 111. In particular, the possible counterexamples up to X number at most O(X^0.406).

*Proof.* The T-trajectory contains the same odd values as the C-trajectory, and 5 and 7 mod 8 are odd classes. So n ∈ E iff T^i(n) is not 5 or 7 mod 8 for every i. By the Terras bijection (see CLS-066.md, Theorem 2), T^i(n) = 5 mod 8 iff v_i v_{i+1} v_{i+2} = 100, and T^i(n) = 7 mod 8 iff that factor is 111. So n ∈ E forces its length-L parity word to avoid {100, 111}. That leaves at most a_L classes mod 2^L, each with at most 2 members in [1,X].

Counting: let g(m) be the number of such words of length m that start with 1. Then g(1), g(2), g(3) = 1, 2, 2. For m >= 4, g(m) = g(m-2) + g(m-3). The reason: after the leading 1, a 0 must be followed by 1 (100 is banned), and 11 must be followed by 01 (111 and then 100 are banned). The word is therefore 1 0 [word starting with 1 of length m-2] or 1 1 0 [word starting with 1 of length m-3], and no forbidden factor crosses the junction because both forbidden factors start with 1. By induction, g(m) <= 2ρ^{m-1}, with base cases 1 <= 2, 2 <= 2ρ and 2 <= 2ρ^2, and step ρ^{m-1} = ρ^{m-3} + ρ^{m-4}. A word is 0^j followed by a word starting with 1, or is all zeros, so a_L = 1 + Σ_{m=1}^{L} g(m) <= 1 + 2(ρ^L - 1)/(ρ - 1) <= 1 + 6.17 ρ^L. Finally ρ^L <= X^{log2 ρ}. ∎

## Proposition 3 (the conjecture with one more forbidden class)
Each of the following strengthened avoidance statements is PROVED: every C-trajectory that does not reach 1 contains some m in K, where

- (a) K = {5, 7 mod 8} ∪ {57 mod 64}, or
- (b) K = {5, 7 mod 8} ∪ {1 mod 32}.

*Proof.* Use YAH Prop. 4.15 (proved there; re-checked in census/round2/literature/check_yah_avoid57.py). A trajectory that avoids {5,7} and never reaches 1 eventually yields an infinite orbit of G, where G(n) = (3n+1)/4 on 1 mod 32 and G(n) = (9n+7)/8 on 57 mod 64. Every point of that G-orbit is a point of the C-trajectory.
- (a) If 57 mod 64 is also avoided, only the branch A(n) = (3n+1)/4 is used. For n > 1, A(n) < n, so the orbit strictly decreases, which is impossible for an infinite orbit of integers > 1 (the orbit point 1 means the trajectory reached 1).
- (b) If 1 mod 32 is also avoided, only the branch B(n) = (9n+7)/8 is used. B(n) + 7 = 9(n+7)/8, so r consecutive B-steps need 8^r | n+7, and the orbit is finite. ∎

So the open core of Conj. 4.14 is precisely the infinite interleaving of both G-branches, as the round-2 notes say.

## Relation to other items
CLS-066 (up_ct3) is exactly YAH Conj. 4.13 (class 7 alone) and implies CLS-056. CLS-056 is the weaker statement, and YAH call it "easier". It is still a stated $500 prize problem, and their automated search failed on it.

## What was not achieved

## Referee
**Verdict: CONFIRMED (PARTIAL; the conjecture stays open). One novelty caveat applies.**
- Theorem 1 re-derived. Take the last odd m > 1 before the first 1. Then 3m+1 = 4^k with k >= 2, and (4^k-1)/3 = 5 mod 8. Correct, and the converse is trivial. Independent test with the census step `yah_avoid57`: E ∩ [1,2^18] is exactly {2^0..2^18}.
- Theorem 2 re-derived. 5 mod 8 <-> 100 and 7 mod 8 <-> 111. The recurrence g(m) = g(m-2)+g(m-3) holds; there are no junction factors, since both banned words start with 1. The induction gives g(m) <= 2ρ^{m-1}, and 2/(ρ-1) = 6.16. I checked 2 a_L <= 2 + 12.4 X^{log2 ρ} for X up to 2*10^5, and the exhaustive class count = a_L for L = 3..14. The JSON's "≈ X^0.4057" should read "O(X^0.4057)", because it is an upper bound.
- Proposition 3 re-derived from scratch, without relying on YAH. For odd n = 1 mod 8, the next odd value is (3n+1)/4 when n = 1 mod 32. When n = 57 mod 64 it is a 3 mod 8 value followed by (9n+7)/8 = 1 mod 8. Every other class hits 5 or 7 mod 8 within two odd steps. I checked this exhaustively for odd n = 1 mod 8 below 2^16. Parts (a) and (b) are then correct.
  - **Novelty caveat:** (a) and (b) are a repackaging of item 3 of census/round2/literature/yah_avoid57.md, which already proves each B-run finite (8^r | n+7) and notes that A contracts. They are stated cleanly here but are not new content. The same goes for "CLS-066 implies CLS-056", which is immediate.
- Nothing here depends on an open conjecture, and no simulation is passed off as proof. The authors' check passes in 1.7 s.
