Finite certificates for seven Collatz-like systems

Question and scope

For a map T and positive seed n, a first-step separator is a set C with
T(C) contained in C, n outside C, and T(n) in C. Induction excludes every
positive-time return to n. A periodic separator uses a union of whole
residue classes modulo one positive integer M. A basin label instead has
L(T(n)) = L(n) and describes the eventual cycle.

1. Existence of periodic first-step separators at arbitrary modulus

For the 1932 permutation and every split map in the census, there is no
periodic first-step separator at any positive seed. For the parity map
T(even n)=n/2, T(odd n)=a*n+d, with a=3 or 5, the exact criteria are

  gcd(a,d)=1:    a divides the seed;
  d=a:           the exponent of a in the seed differs from 1.

These cover all the offsets in the search, including d=-1 for a=3.
The assertions concern separation from the first image. A finite transient
orbit, for example, can have a binary automaton certificate even when this
periodic criterion fails.

2. Removing the binary part of a modulus along forward paths

Write M=2^k*m with m odd. A full fiber above z means all residues modulo M
congruent to z modulo m. For the parity map use two legitimate blocks:
H(n)=n/2 on even inputs and V(n)=(a*n+d)/2 on odd inputs (one odd step and
one halving). For the split map with prime p=1 mod 4 use H, and on the
two odd classes use (3n+p)/4 and (n+p)/4 (one odd step and two halvings).

All these blocks have odd numerator slope and denominator 2^b; the branch
is specified by b low bits. Start from any representative and follow blocks
until the accumulated exponent L is at least k. Changing the starting value
by 2^L*m*t preserves its residue mod M and the entire branch word. The
endpoint changes by A*m*t for odd A. Varying t through nonnegative
integers produces every binary residue above that endpoint modulo m.
Thus every nonempty forward-closed residue set contains a full fiber
reachable from each of its vertices. From a full fiber, ANY of the blocks
propagates another full fiber: choose its b low input bits and then vary
the input by 2^b*m*t. Division on odd moduli below means multiplication
by the modular inverse.

3. Parity maps: proof of the criterion

First assume gcd(a,d)=1. On multiples of a, each halving preserves
divisibility by a, and the first odd step leaves it forever. For a given
seed divisible by a, write t=v_2(n). Accept all integers coprime to a
and all multiples of a with binary valuation strictly less than t.
This is closed, separates the seed, and uses modulus a*2^t, including t=0.

For the converse take a seed coprime to a. Write the odd part of the
proposed modulus as m=a^e*u with gcd(u,a)=1. On u the normalized maps H
and V are permutations: their finite semigroup is a group on each of its
orbits. On a^e both maps preserve units. There is a word sending any
unit to any specified unit, while simultaneously allowing any desired
endpoint in the same group orbit modulo u, as follows.

If e>0, V^e sends every starting residue modulo a^e to the same unit c:
its slope contains a^e, and its final residue modulo a is d/2. Then H^j
sends c to the target unit for a suitable j, because 2 generates the units
modulo every power of 3 or 5. Indeed 2 has order a-1 modulo a and
2^(a-1)-1 contains exactly one factor of a; the binomial expansion shows
that raising to the a-th power increases this valuation by exactly one at
each level. For e=0 use the empty suffix. This chosen suffix is a
permutation modulo u. Prepend a word reaching its inverse image of the
desired endpoint modulo u. The suffix erases that prefix's effect on the
a-primary coordinate. This proves the simultaneous steering assertion.

Now put the first image T(n) into any forward-closed residue set. It is a
unit modulo a if e>0, and its u-coordinate lies in the group orbit of n.
For an odd seed this last fact follows from T(n)=2*V(n), since multiplication
by 2 is the inverse of H on u. Section 2 supplies a reachable full fiber;
its coordinates still satisfy those two conditions. Steering full fibers
as above reaches the fiber containing n. Every closed set containing T(n)
therefore contains n.

For d=a, the multiples of a form a closed set. An input outside this set
enters it at its first odd step. The same binary-valuation construction
as above (with the roles of multiples and nonmultiples reversed) separates
every such input. On multiples of a, the exact conjugacy
T_(a,a)(a*x)=a*T_(a,1)(x) applies. A periodic separator for x lifts by
scaling to one for a*x; restriction to multiples and rescaling takes a
periodic separator in the other direction. The preceding criterion now
gives the assertion about exponent 1.

4. Split maps: all forward-closed periodic sets

For prime p=1 mod 4 in this family (hence p>=5), all such sets are unions
of zero and multiplicative cosets of <2,3> modulo p. Each is conserved
in both directions of membership across a step. Here is the converse.
For a closed set modulo M, let F be its full fibers over the odd part m.
Section 2 shows F is closed under H and the two normalized odd branches.
It is also closed under multiplication by 2 on m, since H is a permutation
of finite order. Undoing the divisions by 4 shows that F is closed under
x -> x+p and x -> 3x+p. Translation by p gives whole classes modulo
g=gcd(m,p); on g the induced multipliers 2 and 3 are invertible. Hence
F consists of whole orbits of these multipliers modulo g. Every vertex
of the original closed set reaches a full fiber without changing its
multiplier orbit modulo g. Its own fiber is therefore full. This proves
the classification; g is either 1 or p. Direct substitution in each branch
proves that all the listed sets are conserved.

For every positive start the odd subsequence descends into [1,p]: the next
odd term is at most (3n+p)/4. Within [1,p] the same bound stays <=p.
The exported odd-core arrays, indexed by (n-1)/2 for odd n<=p, give the
minimum of the eventual cycle and the exact number of odd-to-odd steps
before first entering it. All odd-core labels and ranks are finite evidence
which can be checked against the three defining branches. This classifies
basins even when distinct cycles occupy the same modular coset.

5. Original 1932 permutation

Let M=3^j*m with 3 not dividing m. For each input, fixing its first j
ternary branch choices is ensured by fixing its residue modulo 3^j
(induct on j). Changing it by M*t changes the j-th iterate by 2^h*m*t
for some h, with those branches fixed. This covers a full fiber modulo
3^j above a single m-coordinate. From a full fiber any branch can be
chosen; starting differences 3*m*t propagate to 2*m*t or 4*m*t and
again give full ternary fibers.

On m the branch maps 2x/3, (4x-1)/3 and (4x+1)/3 are all available
from each residue. Write m=2^b*u with u coprime to 6. On u all branches
are permutations. Composing the last with the inverse of the middle one
gives translation by 2/3, transitive on u. For a proposed target modulo
m, take a positive representative and follow b inverse steps of the
integer permutation; keep the resulting forward branch word as a suffix.
Its numerator slope contains 2^b. Applied as affine residue maps on m,
it forces the desired binary coordinate regardless of the suffix input
and permutes the u-coordinate. A prefix can reach the required inverse
image on u, so the target is reachable. The graph modulo m is strongly
connected. Propagation of full ternary fibers now gives the same conclusion
on M. This recovers the fixed-modulus argument in collatz_8_handoff/HANDOFF.md.

6. How to reproduce and interpret the finite searches

Run python3 certificate_search.py --json output/certificate_search.json
and python3 verify_certificate_search.py output/certificate_search.json.
The default parameters are all odd primes <=997, all moduli <=128 plus
p,2p,4p in parameterized maps, and up to three accessible binary states
after the leading 1. The extra start condition for positive canonical
binary input is implicit. Nonconstant languages are represented once,
as minimal automata numbered in breadth-first order. Accepting states
are the set bits of accepting_mask; identifiers index the machines array
starting at zero.

For modulus M, a branch modulus B of 2, 3 or 4 makes B*M consecutive
inputs a complete edge check. In the directed residue graph every closed
set is a union of strongly connected components that contains all its
successors. An edge distinguishes its endpoints by a forward-closed set
iff it crosses components. The reachable set from its endpoint is the
smallest such certificate. The output records moduli with any nontrivial
closed set, with a crossing edge, and with a crossing at the designated
seed, including a witness for its first successful modulus.
The separate periodic_witness field uses the all-moduli theorem: accept n
if scale divides n and x=n/scale satisfies either the indicated prime
divisibility condition or binary_divisor does not divide x.

Binary closure is checked on every affine branch (u*q+r) -> (v*q+s)
for all q>=0. A small sample discards candidates with a concrete bad edge.
For survivors, putting q=2t+b replaces the left offset by floor((u*b+r)/2)
and pulls the accepting mask back through the discarded binary digit;
the same operation applies to the right offset and the rejection mask.
Testing the remaining quotient zero and exploring both bits exhausts all
q. The offsets are bounded by the maximum of their original value and
slope, and there are finitely many state masks. Skipping leading zeros
uses a separate padding state. Thus the state bound limits the machines
searched but no trajectory cutoff is used in validating their closure.

For an exact repeating trace, terms lists the distinct states in order;
the successor of the last is terms[repeat_index]. An index of zero gives
a period of the seed. A positive index makes the listed states after the
seed a finite forward-closed certificate. Unfinished traces have a stop
reason and a number of checked terms. These counts alone certify neither
divergence nor a later return.
